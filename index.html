The First Hours After Water Damage

A burst pipe at 2 in the morning does not wait for office hours, and mold does not wait either. Mold starts in 24 to 48 hours on a wet wall, so the first hours decide how big the job gets. The crew that answers the phone at night is the crew that saves the drywall, the flooring, and the furniture. For a full rundown of the emergency service, see emergency mold remediation orlando on the service page.

Turn off the water first. Find the main shutoff valve, usually near the water meter or in the garage, and close it. Put towels around the wet baseboard and set a bucket under a ceiling drip. Take photos of every wet spot, every stain, and every swollen board, with the time on your phone. Photos are your insurance record. Do not mop the whole area yet. Mopping spreads spores across a dry floor. Do not run the air conditioner at full blast. The air system pulls spores from a wet room into every bedroom.

When to Call Right Away

Call right away when water is still running, when the ceiling sags, when the smell is strong in every room, or when anyone in the home has severe asthma. Call right away when the water came from a burst pipe, a failed appliance hose, or a roof leak in a storm. Call right away when the wet area is larger than 10 square feet. A crew that arrives in the first hours can dry the structure before mold takes hold. A crew that arrives after a week finds a full removal job instead of a drying job.

Do not wait to see if it dries on its own. In Florida humidity, a wet wall stays wet. A wet wall behind a closed door stays wet for weeks. The longer the wait, the more drywall must be cut, the more flooring must be lifted, and the higher the bill runs. A call in the first hour costs nothing. A call after a week costs a rebuild.

How the Emergency Response Runs

The crew arrives with moisture meters, a thermal camera, and extraction equipment. The assessor maps the moisture in walls, floors, and ceilings, and writes a scope before any cutting starts. The crew extracts standing water, sets dehumidifiers and air movers, and seals the work zone with six mil poly sheeting. A HEPA air scrubber rated at 500 cubic feet per minute keeps spores from drifting into clean rooms. Contaminated drywall is cut 12 to 24 inches past the visible edge and bagged inside containment. Wood is HEPA vacuumed and treated with a botanical antimicrobial. Drying runs three to five days until lumber drops below 15 percent moisture.

Florida law keeps the assessor apart from the removal crew, so the scope cannot be used to sell a bigger job. An independent assessor returns after drying to test the air and confirm Condition 1 normal ecology. Only after clearance does the rebuild start.

After the Emergency Visit

Keep every photo, every receipt, and the written scope. Send the insurer the photos, the scope, and the lab report. Insurance pays only for sudden and accidental water events, like a burst pipe or a failed appliance hose. A slow leak is classed as maintenance and is usually denied. Most Florida policies also carry a mold sub limit that caps the payout. A claim with photos and a written scope is paid faster than a claim with a phone call alone.

Fix the water source before the rebuild. A new wall over a leaking pipe is a new mold job in six months. Check the roof flashing, the appliance hoses, and the air conditioner drain line. Walk the home twice a year and look for new stains, damp baseboards, and musty smells. Small fixes in spring cost far less than an emergency call in August.
